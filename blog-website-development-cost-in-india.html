Website Development Cost in India in 2026: What Should You Actually Pay?

"How much will my website cost?" is the first question every business owner asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on what the website needs to do. This guide gives you realistic ballpark ranges so you can judge any quote you receive.

By Web Shakti Team 14 Nov 2026 3 min read

Typical price ranges in 2026

These are general market ranges across India, not a quote. Prices vary by city, by company and by how much custom work is involved.

Type of projectGood forBallpark (₹)
Simple 3 to 5 page websiteClinics, salons, mehndi artists, coaching classes, local shops6,000 – 25,000
Business website with gallery, blog and SEO setupService companies, schools, banquet halls, agencies25,000 – 60,000
E-commerce storeSelling products online with payments and orders40,000 – 1,50,000+
Custom web application with admin panelStudent management, billing, bookings, dashboards60,000 – 3,00,000+
Mobile app (Android / iOS)Repeat customers, loyalty, push notifications1,00,000 – 5,00,000+

What makes a website cost more

  • Number of pages. Every page needs design, content and testing.
  • Custom design vs ready template. A design made for your brand takes more hours than a modified template.
  • Features. Online booking, payments, user login, Excel upload and admin panels all add development time.
  • Content. Professional photos, written copy and translations are often extra.
  • SEO work. Proper titles, descriptions, schema, sitemap and speed tuning take effort but pay back for years.
  • Integrations. WhatsApp, Google Maps, CRM, payment gateways and SMS.

Costs people forget

The build price is not the whole story. Plan for these too:

  • Domain name: renewed every year, usually under ₹1,500 for a .in or .com.
  • Hosting: a yearly fee that depends on traffic and the type of site.
  • Maintenance and support: updates, backups and security checks. Many businesses take an annual maintenance contract (AMC) for this.
  • Content updates: new offers, photos and pages after launch.

How to avoid overpaying

  1. Write down what the website must do before you ask for quotes. Three clear goals beat twenty vague ideas.
  2. Ask for a scope in writing: pages, features, revisions, delivery date and what is not included.
  3. Start small and grow. Launch with the pages that bring enquiries, then add features once you see results.
  4. Compare value, not only price. Check mobile speed, SEO basics and support, not just the final number.

Warning signs of a "too cheap" quote: no mention of mobile design, no SEO basics, you do not own the domain or source files, and nobody to call after launch.

So what should you pay?

If you run a local business and need to be found on Google and contacted on WhatsApp, a clean 5 to 8 page website with SEO basics is usually enough to start. If you need logins, billing or an admin panel, you are looking at a custom application and the price moves into the higher ranges above.
